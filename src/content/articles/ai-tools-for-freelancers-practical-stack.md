Most freelancers don't need a dozen AI subscriptions. They need a few tools that reliably save time on the tasks they repeat every week — and that don't create problems with client confidentiality.

## Start with your work, not the tools

List the tasks you do most often in a typical week and roughly how long each takes. Common candidates:

- Writing and editing (proposals, emails, deliverables)
- Summarizing documents, calls or research
- Planning and admin (scheduling, invoicing notes, task lists)
- Creative work (images, slides, social content)

Circle the two or three tasks where a faster first draft would genuinely help. That is where an AI tool earns its place.

## Evaluate each candidate on five criteria

| Criterion | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Fit | Does it handle *your* task well, tested on a realistic, non-confidential sample? |
| Cost | What does it cost per month, and what is the free tier's real limit? |
| Privacy | How does the vendor handle your inputs? Can you opt out of training? |
| Export | Can you get your work out easily if you switch? |
| Overlap | Does a tool you already pay for do this well enough? |

## Keep the stack small

A practical starting point is one general-purpose assistant plus, at most, one specialist tool for your highest-value task. Add more only when you can name the hours it saves.

## Review it monthly

Put a recurring reminder in your calendar. Cancel anything you didn't use, and re-check vendor terms for changes.
