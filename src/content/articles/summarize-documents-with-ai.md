A useful summary is not simply a shorter document. It preserves the claims, caveats, evidence and decisions that matter to the reader. AI can accelerate the first pass, but a safe workflow makes it easy to verify what was included and what may have been missed.

## Prepare the document

Check that the file is readable and complete. Scanned PDFs may need OCR, and tables or footnotes can be lost during extraction. If the document contains confidential client data, confirm that your contract and the tool's current data policy permit uploading it. Where possible, remove names and other identifying details before processing.

## Ask for a structured summary

Instead of prompting “summarize this,” specify the audience, purpose and output. Ask for: the central question, five key findings, important evidence, assumptions, risks or limitations, decisions required, and action items with owners or dates when explicitly stated. Instruct the model to label missing information as “not stated” rather than guessing.

## Work in sections for very long files

For a long report, summarize each chapter separately and ask for a final synthesis across the section summaries. Keep section names or page references so you can trace claims back to the source. A two-stage process is often easier to audit than a single huge prompt, especially when a report contains conflicting findings or technical appendices.

## Verify the critical claims

Open the original document and check every number, date, quotation, recommendation and exception that could affect a decision. Ask the AI to provide page or section references only if it can reliably ground them in the supplied document; then verify those references yourself. Never assume a fluent sentence is a faithful sentence.

## A reusable prompt

“Summarize the supplied document for [audience] who need to [decision or task]. Return: (1) a 100-word overview, (2) key findings with supporting page or section references where available, (3) assumptions and limitations, (4) decisions or actions explicitly stated, and (5) questions the document does not answer. Do not add outside facts. If something is unclear or absent, say so. Separate direct statements from your interpretation.”

## When a summary is not enough

Do not rely on an AI summary alone for legal commitments, medical decisions, financial obligations, safety instructions or other high-stakes matters. Read the relevant source passages in full and consult a qualified professional where needed.

**Bottom line:** Use AI to find the important passages faster; use the original document to confirm that they mean what the summary says.
