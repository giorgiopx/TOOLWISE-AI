AI summaries are fast, but a confident summary can quietly drop the one clause or number that matters. This workflow keeps the speed while reducing that risk.

## 1. Decide what the summary is for

Before you start, write one sentence describing the decision or action the summary should support — for example, "Decide whether to accept the revised project scope." That sentence becomes part of your prompt.

## 2. Check whether you're allowed to share it

If the document belongs to a client, confirm that your agreement and the tool's data terms allow you to upload it. When in doubt, don't.

## 3. Summarize in sections

Long documents often exceed what a tool handles reliably in one pass. Split the document by its own headings and summarize each section, then ask for an overall summary of the section summaries.

## 4. Ask for structure and references

Request a fixed format: key points, numbers and dates, obligations or risks, and open questions — each with the section or page it came from. References make checking far quicker.

## 5. Verify against the source

Spot-check every number, date and obligation against the original. Read in full any section the summary flags as a risk.

## 6. Keep a short record

Note which tool you used, the date and what you verified. If the summary informs client work, that record is useful later.
