Updating a useful page can be more valuable than publishing another broad article. AI can help identify outdated sections, simplify explanations and propose a new structure, but it cannot know which details changed unless you provide current evidence.

## Audit the existing page first

Review traffic and reader intent where reliable analytics are available. Identify sections that answer the question well, sections that are obsolete and information that still deserves to be preserved. Do not rewrite a page just to make it look new; define the reader problem the update should solve.

## Identify change-sensitive claims

Product pricing, feature availability, interface steps, legal requirements and statistics can become outdated. Create a list of claims to recheck and find current primary sources. Note the date of verification when the information is likely to change again.

## Use AI for diagnosis and structure

Ask the model to flag repetition, unexplained jargon, unanswered reader questions and claims that need fresh evidence. Then provide verified sources and ask for a revised outline. Keep a record of major changes so an editor can compare the old and new page.

## Preserve trust and search intent

Do not add irrelevant sections just to increase word count. Keep useful explanations and URLs where possible, and redirect only when a URL genuinely changes and a relevant replacement exists. Avoid adding unsupported claims or fake “updated” labels that imply research that did not occur.

## Review the final page

Check every changed factual statement, internal link, screenshot and step-by-step instruction. Test the page on mobile and ensure the title and description accurately describe the content. If the update changes the promise of the page, make sure the rest of the site links to it appropriately.

**Bottom line:** AI can accelerate the audit and rewrite. Current evidence and editorial judgement determine whether the refreshed page is genuinely better.

> Editorial standard: This guide provides general workflow advice and does not claim hands-on product testing. Verify changing product-specific, legal, tax or pricing details against current primary sources before adding them.
