A content brief is a decision document, not a list of keywords. It explains who the piece is for, what question it answers, what evidence it needs and how it will be more useful than a generic overview. AI can help assemble the brief, but the angle and evidence plan need editorial judgement.

## Define the reader and task

Describe the reader in practical terms: what they know, what they are trying to do and what decision they need to make. Avoid broad labels such as “everyone interested in AI.” A brief for a solo designer choosing an image workflow is different from a brief for an operations manager reviewing data governance.

## State the search intent

Write the likely question behind the query and what a successful answer enables the reader to do. Identify whether the user wants an explanation, comparison, walkthrough, template or decision framework. Search intent should shape the article structure; a keyword should not be repeated mechanically just to meet an arbitrary density.

## Build an evidence plan

List claims that need verification, primary sources to consult, screenshots or examples to create, and any product details that may change. If the article compares tools, decide what criteria will be used and how each criterion will be checked. Never tell an AI writer to fabricate first-hand testing or pretend that an example is a real result.

## Create a differentiated outline

Ask AI for several possible angles, then reject those that merely restate common advice. A distinctive article might include a decision tree, a worked example, a failure mode, a reusable template or a transparent comparison methodology. Add headings only when they answer a reader question.

## A reusable brief prompt

“Create a content brief for [audience] trying to [task]. Include search intent, reader questions, recommended angle, outline, primary-source research needs, claims that require verification, example or template to create, internal links, and a clear definition of success. Identify what would make this piece meaningfully more useful than a generic AI-written overview. Do not invent data or claim first-hand testing.”

## Quality gate before drafting

A brief is ready when the reader, task, angle and evidence requirements are clear. If those are missing, drafting will likely produce a longer version of what already exists. Review the brief with someone who understands the subject before generating the article.

**Bottom line:** Use AI to explore possibilities, then use editorial judgement to choose the question, evidence and point of view.

> Editorial standard: This guide provides general workflow advice and does not claim hands-on product testing. Verify changing product-specific, legal, tax or pricing details against current primary sources before adding them.
