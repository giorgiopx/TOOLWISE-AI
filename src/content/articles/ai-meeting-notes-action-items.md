Meeting notes are useful only when people can find the decisions and act on them. AI can turn rough notes into a cleaner record, but it should not assign commitments that nobody made or turn a suggestion into an agreed decision.

## Capture the source accurately

Start with your notes or an approved transcript. Check speaker labels and remove irrelevant personal information. If you plan to upload a recording or transcript, make sure you have the required permission and that the tool is appropriate for the information involved. Meeting content may include commercially sensitive material.

## Separate decisions from discussion

Ask the AI to produce five sections: decisions made, action items, open questions, risks or blockers, and topics deferred. For each action item, include the owner and due date only when they are explicitly stated. Otherwise write “owner not assigned” or “date not set.” This small instruction prevents false certainty.

## Use a clear output format

A useful action table contains task, owner, due date, dependency and source note. Keep the source note short enough to help a reader verify the item. Avoid asking for a summary that sounds polished but removes disagreement, conditions or unresolved questions.

## Check the output against the notes

Verify names, dates, amounts and commitments. Review any sentence that uses words such as “approved,” “will,” “must” or “agreed.” Those words can create a misleading record if the original discussion was tentative. If the meeting had a decision-maker, ask that person to confirm the final summary when the stakes justify it.

## A reusable prompt

“Convert these notes into a meeting record. Separate confirmed decisions, explicit action items, open questions, risks and deferred topics. Include an owner or due date only when the notes state one; otherwise label it unassigned or not specified. Do not infer agreement. Quote or reference the source note for ambiguous items. Finish with a concise follow-up email draft.”

## Keep the workflow lightweight

Use a consistent template for recurring meetings and store the final record where the team already works. Do not create a new tool or process unless it makes follow-through easier. For confidential meetings, follow organizational policies and the data controls of the chosen service.

**Bottom line:** AI can organize the record; people remain responsible for confirming what was actually decided.
