AI can help produce a first draft, but publication quality depends on what happens before and after generation. A trustworthy article answers a real question, supports its claims, explains limitations and gives the reader something useful to do.

## Check the reader promise

Compare the headline, introduction and conclusion. Do they promise the same outcome? Remove inflated wording such as “ultimate” or “revolutionary” unless the piece truly supports it. A specific promise helps the right reader decide whether to continue.

## Verify facts and examples

Check names, dates, numbers, product features, quotations and links against reliable sources. Replace fictional examples that could be mistaken for real customer outcomes with clearly labeled hypothetical scenarios. Do not invent hands-on tests, credentials or testimonials.

## Add value beyond the summary

Look for a useful decision framework, worked example, original checklist, explanation of a trade-off or insight from real experience. If the article only restates information available everywhere, it may need a narrower angle or more evidence rather than more words.

## Improve the writing

Remove repeated introductions, generic transitions and paragraphs that do not move the answer forward. Use headings that describe the point of each section. Prefer concrete verbs and explain technical terms. Keep caveats near the claims they qualify rather than burying them at the end.

## Review commercial transparency

If a page contains affiliate links or another commercial relationship, disclose it clearly near the relevant recommendation. Do not allow commissions to determine the editorial conclusion. Include limitations and alternatives where useful.

## Pre-publication checklist

- The article answers a specific reader question.
- Important claims have been checked against appropriate sources.
- Examples are real and authorized, or clearly marked hypothetical.
- No fake testing, quotes, credentials or results appear.
- The structure is easy to scan and the advice is actionable.
- Affiliate relationships and limitations are disclosed.
- Links, mobile layout and metadata work correctly.

**Bottom line:** AI is a drafting aid. The publisher remains responsible for accuracy, originality, transparency and usefulness.

> Editorial standard: This guide provides general workflow advice and does not claim hands-on product testing. Verify changing product-specific, legal, tax or pricing details against current primary sources before adding them.
