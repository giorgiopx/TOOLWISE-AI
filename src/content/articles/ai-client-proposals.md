A good proposal shows that you understand the client's problem, can deliver a defined outcome and have a sensible plan for getting there. AI can help organize the first draft, but the strongest proposal still depends on your judgement about scope, risk and fit.

## Start with a brief extraction

Before drafting, list the client's stated goals, deliverables, constraints, timeline, stakeholders and unanswered questions. Separate what the client explicitly said from what you infer. If the brief is incomplete, create a short clarification list instead of allowing the model to fill gaps with confident guesses.

## Use a proposal structure

Ask AI to organize the material into: understanding of the problem, recommended approach, deliverables, timeline and milestones, client responsibilities, assumptions and exclusions, investment and payment terms, and next steps. Supply your own approved service descriptions and pricing rules. Do not let the model invent case studies, testimonials, certifications or results.

## Make the scope concrete

Vague promises create disputes. Replace “improve the website” with defined deliverables such as a specified number of page designs, revision rounds, handoff files or testing steps. Make dependencies and exclusions explicit. AI can identify ambiguous phrases, but you must decide what you are actually willing to deliver.

## Keep pricing under your control

If you have a pricing formula, provide it as a rule and verify the arithmetic independently. Do not ask a language model to guess what a project should cost from a short description. Consider effort, complexity, risk, revisions, usage rights, taxes and payment terms according to your own business practice.

## A useful prompt

“Using only the client brief and the service details below, draft a proposal. Mark unknown details as [NEEDS CONFIRMATION]. Do not invent credentials, results, client names, deadlines or prices. Include deliverables, exclusions, milestones, dependencies and five questions that should be resolved before acceptance. Keep the tone direct, specific and collaborative.”

## Review before sending

Check every factual claim, date, price and deliverable. Confirm that the proposal reflects your actual availability and contract terms. Read it from the client's perspective: can they tell what they will receive, when they will receive it, what you need from them and what happens if scope changes?

**Bottom line:** Let AI improve structure and clarity. Keep the commercial judgement, commitments and final approval with you.
