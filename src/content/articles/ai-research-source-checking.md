AI can help turn a broad research question into a plan, summarize documents and compare perspectives. It can also produce plausible claims without adequate evidence. The safest approach treats the model as a research assistant whose work must be traced to sources.

## Turn the topic into questions

Break the question into smaller, answerable parts. Identify what is factual, what is interpretive and what depends on current information. For product features, pricing, laws, policies or recent events, use current primary sources rather than relying on model memory.

## Start with source quality

Prefer original documentation, official policies, primary studies and direct statements from the relevant organization. Secondary reporting can provide context, but verify important claims at the source when possible. Record publication dates and the date you accessed changing information.

## Ask for evidence, not confidence

Prompt the model to list claims separately from sources, identify uncertainties and flag statements it cannot substantiate. Then open the cited pages yourself. A URL in an answer does not prove that the page supports the claim; sources can be irrelevant, stale or misrepresented.

## Keep a claim ledger

For each important claim, record the wording, source, date, confidence and what remains unresolved. Distinguish direct evidence from inference. If two reliable sources disagree, preserve the disagreement instead of asking the AI to force a single neat conclusion.

## Watch for common failure modes

Be cautious with precise statistics lacking a traceable source, quotations that cannot be found in the original, outdated feature descriptions, overly broad claims based on one example and citations that lead to a general homepage. These are reasons to investigate, not proof that every AI answer is wrong.

## A useful research prompt

“Help me research [question]. Break it into subquestions. For each, identify what evidence would answer it, which primary sources to seek, what could change over time, and what uncertainty remains. Do not invent citations. Separate established facts, interpretations and open questions. I will verify sources before publication.”

## Before publishing

Check the strongest claims, verify numbers and dates, quote accurately, and explain limitations. For consequential legal, medical or financial matters, seek qualified expertise rather than relying on AI-generated synthesis.

**Bottom line:** Use AI to structure the investigation. Let verifiable sources—not fluent wording—determine what you publish.

> Editorial standard: This guide provides general workflow advice and does not claim hands-on product testing. Verify changing product-specific, legal, tax or pricing details against current primary sources before adding them.
