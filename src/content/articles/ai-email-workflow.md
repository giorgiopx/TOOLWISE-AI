AI can make routine email drafting faster, especially when you already know what you need to say. The risk is that a generic draft may sound polished but vague, overconfident or unlike you. A good workflow begins with intent and ends with a human edit.

## Define the job of the email

Before prompting, write one sentence that states the purpose: request a decision, confirm a milestone, explain a delay, summarize next steps or follow up on an invoice. Add the relationship, desired tone and any facts that must remain unchanged. This is more useful than asking for “a professional email.”

## Provide facts, not private context you do not need

Include only information required for the draft. If client names or confidential project details are not necessary, replace them with placeholders. Review the service's current data settings and your obligations before pasting sensitive correspondence into an AI tool.

## Ask for a concise draft

Give the model a word limit or structure. For example: context in one sentence, key message, action required and deadline if known, friendly close. Ask it not to invent dates, commitments, reasons or attachments. If the message is sensitive, request two tone options rather than one supposedly perfect answer.

## Preserve your voice

Build a short style note based on your own writing: direct or warm, short or detailed, formal or conversational, words you prefer and phrases you avoid. Do not upload a large archive of client emails unless you have permission and a clear reason. A concise style guide is usually enough.

## Use a three-pass edit

First, check factual accuracy. Second, remove filler, repetition and overly enthusiastic language. Third, read the email aloud and ask whether you would actually say it. Make the requested action unmistakable and ensure the tone suits the relationship.

## A reusable prompt

“Draft a [tone] email to [recipient role] whose purpose is [goal]. Use only these facts: [facts]. Keep it under [word count] words. Make the next action clear. Do not invent dates, promises, explanations or attachments. Avoid clichés and generic AI phrasing. Use placeholders for any missing details.”

**Bottom line:** AI should reduce the blank-page problem, not remove your judgement or personal voice.
