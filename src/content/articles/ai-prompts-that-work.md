Prompting is less about finding a magic phrase and more about giving a system the information it needs to produce a useful result. Clear context, a defined task, constraints and a reviewable output format usually matter more than a long list of clever adjectives.

## Give the task and context

State what you want to accomplish and who the result is for. Add the relevant background, source material and constraints. Avoid dumping unrelated information into the prompt; too much noise can make the important requirements harder to follow.

## Specify what good looks like

Describe the output format, length, tone, required sections and success criteria. If accuracy matters, tell the model not to invent missing facts and to mark uncertainty explicitly. If the task depends on a source, say whether outside information is allowed.

## Add examples when style matters

One short example can clarify tone or format better than a paragraph of abstract instructions. Use material you have permission to share. Examples should illustrate the pattern, not force the model to copy irrelevant wording or factual content.

## Iterate based on failure

When a result misses the mark, identify the failure precisely: too generic, wrong audience, missing caveat, wrong format or unsupported claim. Change one or two instructions and try again. Save prompts that work for recurring tasks, but review them when the workflow or model changes.

## A flexible prompt formula

**Role or perspective + task + context + constraints + output format + verification rule.** Not every prompt needs all six parts. A simple request may only need the task and format; a high-stakes workflow needs stronger boundaries and checking.

Example: “Turn these anonymized project notes into a client update for a non-technical reader. Use three headings: progress, open questions and next steps. Do not add dates or commitments not present in the notes. Keep it under 180 words and flag any ambiguity at the end.”

## Remember the limits

A better prompt cannot guarantee truth, current information or perfect reasoning. Verify factual outputs, especially where mistakes matter. Use approved tools and avoid including secrets or unnecessary personal data.

**Bottom line:** A strong prompt makes the task and success criteria clear. A strong workflow also checks the answer.
